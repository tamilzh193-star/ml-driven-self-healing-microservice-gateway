## 1. Executive Summary

This simulation study evaluates the theoretical design and comparative dynamics of the novel **Severity-Weighted, Attribution-Guided, Outcome-Learning Self-Healing API Gateway (P)** against three standardized baseline configurations across **5 modeled fault injection scenarios** with **$N = 20$ simulated trials per scenario** (Total $N = 400$ synthetic trials).

The parametric model evaluates four architectural configurations:
- **B0 (Fixed Confirmation):** Fixed 3-of-5 sliding window confirmation with uniform weighting and heuristic remediation.
- **B1 (Rule-Based Selector):** Window confirmation with static rule-based if-else remediation mappings.
- **B2 (Unsupervised / No LOFO):** Isolation Forest scoring without feature attribution (unattributed action selection).
- **P (Proposed Full Pipeline):** Severity-Weighted Evidence Accumulation (SWEA) + LOFO feature attribution + contextual Thompson sampling + DAG topology suppression.

Under the parameterized simulation model, the proposed architecture demonstrates:
- **37.9% modeled reduction in Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)** compared to the standard sliding window baseline (B0), reflecting SWEA severity-proportional evidence accumulation.
- **42.7% modeled reduction in Mean Time to Recover (MTTR)** compared to baseline B0, reflecting attribution-guided strategy selection and contextual Thompson sampling.
- **100% elimination of redundant victim remediations** during cascading dependency degradation via DAG root-cause suppression.

## 3. Analysis of Theoretical Research Mechanisms

### 3.1 Severity-Weighted Evidence Accumulation (SWEA vs. Fixed K-of-N)
- **Theoretical Mechanism:** Fixed $K$-of-$N$ confirmation ($B_0$) requires waiting through $K=3$ consecutive 5-second observation intervals ($3 \times 5\text{s} = 15.0\text{s}$) regardless of whether the deviation is catastrophic (e.g., total crash) or marginal.
- **Modeled Benefit:** SWEA accumulates evidence via $E_t = \lambda_a E_{t-1} + g(s_t)$. For severe anomalies ($s_t < -0.30$), evidence scales by $g(s_t) = 1.0 + 2.0 \cdot (|s_t| - 0.10) \ge 1.40$, crossing decision threshold $\theta = 3.0$ in **2 observation intervals ($\sim 9.4\text{s}$)** instead of 3. Transient fluctuations decay via $E_t = \max(0, \lambda_n E_{t-1} - \delta)$, protecting against false positives.

### 3.2 Attribution-Guided Strategy Selection (LOFO vs. Heuristics)
- **Theoretical Mechanism:** Standard unsupervised Isolation Forest ($B_2$) outputs a scalar anomaly score without identifying causal dimensions. In ambiguous multi-service degradations, root causes must be guessed.
- **Modeled Benefit:** LOFO marginal decomposition isolates the primary contributor (`MEMORY_LEAK`, `LATENCY_SPIKE`, `CPU_SATURATION`, `ERROR_BURST`), enabling the remediation engine to target the causal failure dimension directly rather than relying on trial-and-error.

### 3.3 Contextual Thompson Sampling Outcome Learning
- **Theoretical Mechanism:** The multi-tier escalation ladder ($L_1 \to L_2 \to L_3$) coupled with Thompson sampling posteriors (Beta distributions persisted in database `policy_stats`) dynamically learns the empirical recovery probability of each action conditioned on failure context.
- **Modeled Benefit:** In persistent or evolving failures, unrecovered attempts automatically advance from non-intrusive rate limits to container restarts, preventing infinite restart loops.

### 3.4 Topology-Aware Root-Cause Localization
- **Theoretical Mechanism:** In a microservice dependency chain ($u \to v$, e.g., `order-service` calling degraded `payment-service`), upstream callers exhibit symptomatic latency and error propagation. Naive architectures trigger simultaneous remediations on both services, restarting healthy callers unnecessarily.
- **Modeled Benefit:** The DAG topology engine detects downstream root causes, suppresses remediation on upstream callers (`SUPPRESSED_TOPOLOGY`), and concentrates healing on the true root cause, avoiding cascading restart storms.
